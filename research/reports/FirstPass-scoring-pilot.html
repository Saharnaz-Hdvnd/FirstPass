FirstPass: first scoring pilot

Definition: The extent to which available evidence supports a listing’s identity, material claims, disclosures, and proposed transaction.

Development rubric only. The weights have not been validated. A score is evidence support, not a probability that a seller is honest or that a car is safe.

Step 2 — Review evidence, one case at a time

The snapshot intake is complete; full manual assessment is pending. Every check starts unknown. A VIN, title flag or photo URL is a claim or pointer, not independent verification. No photos, title reports, dealer licenses or vehicle inspections have yet been reviewed in this pilot.

  1. Start with P01. Open its listing URL from the pilot JSON. Record access date, save the actual claims, and distinguish current content from the frozen snapshot.
  2. A: compare VIN/core specifications with an official decoder; verify dealer identity through independent contact and applicable licensing. Leave ownership/possession unknown without adequate evidence.
  3. B and C: compare explicit history and mileage claims with dated vehicle-linked records. Do not interpret a false clean-title flag as proof of a branded title. Reconcile dates and units before calling anything contradictory.
  4. D: identify condition, maintenance, recall, battery and certification claims. Record the precise documents needed. No report or photos establish absence of mechanical defects. No automatic paid reports or seller contact is included.
  5. E: actually view the images, verify vehicle attribution, and record which relevant views are missing. Photo count alone earns no points.
  6. F: inspect written purchase-price conditions and fees. Leave cooperation/payment checks unknown until an actual, documented interaction or proposed transaction exists.
  7. For each check record state, evidence IDs, reason and next action. Supported and contradicted require traceable evidence, dates and a reviewer. An unresolved discrepancy remains unknown. Give each distinct event a primary rule to prevent repeated credit or penalties.
  8. Record minutes spent, unavailable sources, ambiguous rules and any applicability decisions. Repeat for P02–P10 before revising the rules. Keep revisions versioned.

Step 3 — Compute, without false reassurance

T = sum of supported weights; D = sum of contradicted weights; U = sum of unknown weights. Coverage = T + D = 100 − U. T + D + U = 100. All ten currently have T=0, D=0, U=100: not yet verified, not “untrustworthy.” Do not rank these tied, unassessed cases. Verified critical alerts take precedence over ranking.

The separate scorer validates annotations and produces JSON. It does not automatically verify claims. Keep all 22 weights for this pilot; review genuine inapplicability manually before implementing alternate normalized profiles.

What follows the ten-case review

  1. Revise ambiguous rules and freeze the next version. Build 50–100 cases with sparse, ordinary and conflicting evidence; deduplicate VINs and source syndication. This cannot estimate rare-fraud probabilities.
  2. Have two reviewers independently annotate an overlapping subset. Record agreement, disagreements and adjudication separately. Do not supply the first reviewer’s labels to the second.
  3. Compare the same cases/rules using structured data alone versus AI-extracted claims. Measure extraction precision/recall, unsupported assertions, unknown handling and evidence traceability.
  4. Run the exploratory manual-browsing / fixed-score / personalized-screening study with balanced task order and consistent time limits. Predefine worthwhile-listing judgments independently of the score.
  5. Measure time per task, worthwhile listings retained divided by worthwhile available, misleading reassurance among materially conflicting cases, and correct evidence-linked explanations divided by explanations audited. Report denominators and uncertainty.
  6. Vary category weights ±20%, renormalize, compare equal weights and remove sources. Report rank correlation and top-k overlap; report ties and instability rather than forced precision.
